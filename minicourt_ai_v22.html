Referee voice uses your device’s speech voice with a slower, formal umpire-style delivery. Automatic court scanning uses the visible white tape. The umpire engine uses the 2026 Rules of Tennis as its reference, with your agreed mini-court practice rules for bounce counting. For best calibration, use Set court corners and mark the four OUTSIDE corners of the white boundary in order. The app maps that quadrilateral into a normalized court plane to compensate for camera perspective. Ball tracking uses a higher-resolution clean camera feed, two-colour segmentation, shape filtering and frame-to-frame motion continuity. It estimates ground contact from the ball's lower silhouette and trajectory. This remains a single-camera estimate: motion blur, occlusion, shadows and a ball moving toward or away from the camera can still cause errors. A line touch counts as IN. Each confirmed in-court bounce increments the current rally count; a second in-court bounce ends the point as DOUBLE BOUNCE. The line decision uses the perspective-corrected contact location and its distance from the calibrated boundary. A single fixed camera cannot provide certified or guaranteed line calls; use the UNCERTAIN result for ambiguous contacts. If tracking is lost, it waits 8 seconds before ending the point; disappearance alone is not an OUT call. At point end it announces the rally length and resets the rally counter for the next point.

Rule basis: 2026 ITF Rules of Tennis, especially Rules 11–13, 23–25 and 28. This camera assistant cannot reliably judge every official fault (for example foot faults, net touches or hindrance) from one camera, so it only announces events it can observe with sufficient confidence.
